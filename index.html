COMMON QUESTIONS

Before you go solar, you may want to know.

Clear, honest answers to the practical questions homeowners and businesses ask us most often.

Solar panels on your roof convert daylight into direct current (DC) electricity. A grid-tied inverter transforms this into alternating current (AC) for your lights, fans, and appliances. Any surplus electricity generated during peak daylight hours is sent to the electricity board through a bi-directional net meter.

System size depends primarily on your monthly electricity consumption (units on your electricity bill), sanctioned load, and available shadow-free roof area. Typically, a 3 kW to 5 kW system meets the requirements of most typical residential homes in Nagpur.

Yes. Rooftop solar can be mounted on RCC flat roofs, tin/metal sheds, or elevated customized structures designed to retain usable rooftop space. A preliminary inspection ensures your roof receives adequate unshaded sunlight.

Net metering is an arrangement with your electricity distribution company (e.g. MSEDCL). A bi-directional meter records both the energy you import from the grid and the excess solar electricity you export. You only pay for the net units consumed.

A complete turnkey installation includes site survey, system design, mounting structures, solar modules, grid-tied inverter, AC & DC distribution boxes, cabling, lightning arrester, chemical earthing pits, physical installation, testing, and net-metering documentation assistance.

The primary components are high-efficiency Mono PERC solar modules, a certified string inverter, hot-dip galvanized mounting structure, ACDB/DCDB protection boxes, Polycab or equivalent solar-grade cables, and safety earthing hardware. Specific brands are detailed in your project proposal.

The output from the solar inverter passes through an AC protection box and connects to your building's main distribution board and the utility net meter. Shashikala Power Tech coordinates the required documentation and grid sync testing.
